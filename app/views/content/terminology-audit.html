Terminology audit

Comparison of terminology and hint text across the three export document journeys: Catch Certificate (CC), Processing Statement (PS), and Non-Manipulation Document / Storage Document (NMD/SD).

Users complete these journeys in order: CC first, then PS, then NMD/SD.

# Area CC (Catch Certificate) PS (Processing Statement) NMD/SD (Non-Manipulation) Conceptually similar Priority
1 "Species" vs "fish" vs "raw" vs "product" vs "landed state" "species" in hint only — "weight of the species to be exported in its landed state" / "products" used as page name — "Add products" / "landed state" used nowhere else "Species" used as label — "Species weight on catch certificate" / "raw" used in details link only — "weight of the raw fish before any UK processing" / "product" used in hints — "weight of the product before/after processing" "fish" used in hints — "weight of the fish only" / "product" used as label — "Net weight of the product" / "fishery products" used as label — "Net weight of fishery products" All five words are used interchangeably across journeys to describe the same thing: the fish before processing. A single agreed term is needed before anything else can be made consistent. Critical
2 Weight — source document Not present — CC is the originating document "Species weight on catch certificate" / hint: "Enter the weight of the species as shown on the catch certificate." "Weight on entry document" / hint: "Total weight on document for the selected commodity code in kg." PS and NMD both look back at the previous document's weight — this is the same concept described differently. Critical
3 Weight — initial/raw state "Export weight (kg)" / hint: "Enter the weight of the species to be exported in its landed state." "Export weight before processing" / hint: "Enter the weight of the product before any processing. Do not include any additional ingredients." "Net weight of the product on arrival" / hint: "This is the weight of the whole product, including all ingredients – not just the fish. For example, a fish pie weighing 0.35kg." All three describe the weight of the fish as it enters this stage of the supply chain — before any transformation. Critical
4 Weight — fish only "Export weight (kg)" / "Total export weight" — neither field separates fish-only weight from whole product weight "Species weight on catch certificate" / "Export weight before processing" — neither field separates fish-only weight from whole product weight "Net weight of fishery products on arrival" / hint: "This is the weight of the fish only – do not include any other ingredients. For example, 0.2kg of cod used in a fish pie." CC and PS both have weight fields but neither separates fish-only weight from whole product weight. NMD does. This distinction may need to be applied consistently across all three. Critical
5 Weight — processed/final "Total export weight" "Export weight after processing" / hint: "Enter the weight of the product after any processing. Do not include any additional ingredients." "Net weight on departure" / "Fishery product weight on departure" / no hint text on departure summary All three describe the weight at the point the fish or product leaves this stage — after transformation or storage. Critical
6 Weight flow (input vs output) Single weight field, no distinction Three separate weight fields but not labelled as input/output Arrival vs departure split across tabs — clearest of the three The weight journey becomes more complex with each document — only NMD makes the input/output flow visually explicit. Critical
7 Inclusions/exclusions Not mentioned in any hint text "Do not include any additional ingredients" appears in both PS weight hints but does not specify what those ingredients are "not just the fish" / "do not include any other ingredients" / brine, breadcrumbs, sauce, oil explicitly listed in details link Guidance on what counts toward each weight becomes more detailed with each journey but is not consistent — CC has none, PS has partial, NMD has most. Critical
8 Document reference "Your reference" "Catch certificate" / hint: "Enter the catch certificate reference number, including any hyphens, dashes or other characters. For example, GBR-2024-CC-BEFCD6036." "Entry document" / hint: "Enter the entry document reference number, including any hyphens, dashes or other characters. For example, GBR-2024-CC-BEFCD6036." "Catch certificate" (PS) and "Entry document" (NMD) may refer to the same document — NMD uses the broader term as the entry document could also be a PS or NMD. Critical
9 Processing types hint: "This could include filleting, smoking, freezing, battering or canning." (radio button hint) "filleting, smoking, freezing, battering or canning" (details link only) Not mentioned CC and PS reference the same list. NMD does not — but storage may involve some of these processes. High
10 Page titles — adding products/species "What are you exporting?" (add products) / "Add your trip" (manual entry and direct landing) "Add your processed product" (product description) / "Add species to your processed product" (product details) "Product details" (product details) / "Check and confirm your consignment weight" (departure summary) All three journeys have pages for adding product and weight information but the page titles use different framing — "exporting", "processed product", and "product details" describe the same action at different stages. NMD's "Product details" is notably less descriptive than the others. High
11 "State" and "Presentation" "State" and "Presentation" — explicit dropdown fields describing how the fish is prepared (e.g. gutted, filleted, smoked) Users describe this themselves in the free-text "Product description" field (e.g. "Battered cod fillets") "Product storage" — how the product is stored: chilled, frozen or other All three journeys capture information about the condition or preparation of the fish but in very different ways — structured dropdowns (CC), free text (PS), and storage condition (NMD). The concept is similar but the approach and terminology differ significantly. High
12 "Consignment" Not used "Consignment" used in product summary — "You have added X processed product(s) to this consignment" / "Consignment item X" as section header in check your answers "Consignment item X" used as section header in check your answers / "Check and confirm your consignment weight" as page title "Consignment" appears in PS and NMD but not CC. In NMD it is used as a page title, in PS it is used in body text and section headers. CC uses "Products" and "Landings" instead. A decision is needed on whether "consignment" should be used consistently or avoided in favour of simpler language. High
13 "Trip" vs "landing" "Add your trip" (page title for manual entry and direct landing) / "Start date of fishing trip" (field label) / "Your landings" (table heading) / "Add landing" (button) / "Landings" (check your answers section) Neither "trip" nor "landing" used Neither "trip" nor "landing" used A trip is the whole fishing voyage; a landing is the act of bringing fish ashore. One trip can have multiple landings. These are used inconsistently within CC itself — the page is called "Add your trip" but the table shows "Your landings" and the button says "Add landing". High
